What this assumes
Distances are great-circle (shortest path over the earth) in nautical miles, which is what a tape measure in Google Earth gives you.

Every ETA assumes the ship holds the speed you entered and steams straight at the destination. It doesn't know about traffic separation schemes, weather routing, or the ship slowing down — so treat it the way you'd treat MarineTraffic's own ETA, and redo it when you get a fresh position. If she plainly isn't heading for the port, none of this applies and the chart and a ruler are still the answer.

A range ring is measured from the destination, not from wherever you're flying out of, unless you pick the same place for both.
Drag the open end of her track to where she's actually heading, for when she's passing rather than coming in. Her position comes off the Calculate tab, not off this map.

Places

Set these once. Ports are where she may be heading; airfields are where you fly from, and the range rings are measured from the airfield you pick.

Ranges

Rings: one or more, separated by commas — 100, 80 gives you both. A lift time is the latest you can be off the ground at that airfield and still make the ring; getting the machine ready is your own business on top.
